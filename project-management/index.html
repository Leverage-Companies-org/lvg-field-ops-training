Welcome to Project Management.

This side of the team runs our fix and flips from the day a deal goes under contract, through the purchase loan and closing, to the day we sell it. We set the budget, run the renovation, pull lender draws, pay contractors, get the house listing-ready, and close it out.

On a flip, profit is made or lost in two places: finishing on time and finishing on budget. Both start with a clear scope of work. This course teaches you how we do it.

How a flip makes money

BuyGet the deal to the closing table with a purchase and rehab loan from Kiavi, Genesis, or Renovo.
›
RenovateCatalina Contracting, our in-house team, runs the work to a clear scope. Paid through lender draws.
›
Make readyClean, stage, photograph, and list.
›
SellProfit = sale price minus purchase, renovation, holding, and closing costs.
Why tracking matters so much Every untracked receipt, duplicate entry, or overpaid invoice comes straight out of profit. A $500 mistake on 11 open jobs is $5,500 gone. If the tracker is wrong, we don’t actually know whether we made money.

Module 3

The scope of work

The scope of work (SOW) is the plan for the whole flip. The lender funds it, Catalina builds it, draws pay for it, and the tracker measures against it. If the scope is vague, everything after it gets messy.

One scope, four jobs

Gets the loanThe lender approves the rehab budget from our scope.
Directs the crewCatalina builds exactly what the scope says.
Drives the drawsEach draw requests money for finished scope lines.
Measures the jobThe tracker compares actual cost to every scope line.

How we build a scope, step by step

Right after contract

Walk the property

Matt and Jerson walk every room, the exterior, roof, basement, and all systems. Take photos and measurements. Note code items (smoke and CO alarms, handrails, GFCI outlets) and anything that will need a permit.

Write line items by trade

Break the work into lines: demo, framing, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, drywall, kitchen, bath, flooring, paint, exterior, permits. Each line says what, how much, and what materials.

Price every line

Catalina prices its own labor and materials. Licensed trades (electric, plumbing, HVAC, roofing) get a written quote from an outside sub. Include permit fees.

Add contingency

A buffer for surprises behind the walls. Genesis requires 7% when the rehab is over $150K.

Group lines into draw categories and milestones

Tag each line Demo, Structural, MEP, Exterior, Finishes, or Soft Costs, the same categories as QuickBooks and Ramp. Decide which lines must be finished for draw 1, draw 2, and so on.

Matt approves

Excel format, property address at the top, itemized by trade. The total becomes the rehab budget.

Submit with the loan

Uploaded with the loan package. Renovo also wants any prior spend if work already started. If the lender approves a different number, update the scope to match.

Load it into the tracker

Each scope line goes into Section B with its budgeted dollars. The total is the Reno Budget in the header.

Build and draw against it

Jerson runs the crews line by line. The SOW Draw Request sheet tracks each draw: broad line items, amount requested, specific line items, actual cost, date, and the running surplus.

Change it only by change order

Once approved, the scope changes only through a written, priced, Matt-approved change order logged in Section F.

Three numbers to keep straight

Rehab budgetWhat the lender approved from our scope.
Contract priceWhat Catalina and any subs will charge to build it.
Total spentWhat we’ve actually paid so far.

The gap between the rehab budget and the contract price is room for contingency and our own spend. When total spent passes the contract price, we’re in overage.

Change orders

Something changesHidden damage, a different finish, tree removal.
›
Price itIn writing, from Catalina or the sub.
›
Matt approvesNo approval, no work.
›
Log itSection F with cost and schedule impact. Label the invoice “change order.”
This is where overages come from If the scope says $100,000 and invoices reach $108,000, every extra dollar should trace to an approved change order. If it doesn’t, the scope was vague or a change slipped through.

Module 5

Closing the purchase

The contract gives us a deadline. If the loan isn’t ready by then, we can lose the deal or our deposit. Everything in this module is about beating the clock.

Watch three dates in Salesforce

InspectionContingency end date. Our window to back out over condition.
MortgageContingency end date. The loan must be committed before this.
CloseTarget closing date with the seller.
The mortgage contingency is the real deadline If the loan isn’t on track a week before the mortgage contingency ends, tell Matt right away. He may need to request an extension or look at other options.

From submission to keys

Submit

Create the checklist from the lender template. Upload entity docs, contract, scope of work, and bank statements. Pay any deposit.

Appraisal

The lender orders it. We give access: Field Ops meets the appraiser. If the ARV comes in low, tell Matt the same day, because it can shrink the loan.

Title and insurance

Title commitment must show clear title. The insurance binder must list the lender as mortgagee. Get the title, escrow, and insurance agent contacts early.

Term sheet and commitment

Matt reviews rate, LTC, points, term, and draw schedule, then signs. Return it before the deadline.

Clear PTD conditions

Every prior-to-docs item must be green before the lender issues closing documents. Check the portal daily.

Draw setup

Enter the draw bank account (entity name must match the loan). Learn the lender’s draw process. Get the inspector contact.

Closing

Confirm the date with title, lender, and seller’s attorney. Final walkthrough with photos. Review the closing disclosure. Verify the wire. Sign. Record the deed.

File it

Signed loan docs to the Loan Docs folder. HUD, loan docs, COF, and EIN linked on the Portfolio Tracker. Then deal setup begins.

Wire safety

Never trust wire instructions from email Wire fraud targets real estate closings. Always confirm wire instructions by calling the title company at a number you already know. Not a number in the email. If instructions ever “change,” stop and call Matt.

Front end and back end

In Salesforce, a flip usually has two records. The front-end record is our purchase from the seller. The back-end record (named “Back-End ~ address”) is our sale to the end buyer. Some properties also carry a “HOLDINGS” record. Make sure expenses and dates go on the right one.

Module 7

Lender draws

The lender doesn’t hand over the renovation money up front. We do the work, prove it, and get reimbursed in pieces called draws. Draws keep the job funded.

The draw cycle

Work gets done

A phase is finished on site. Take dated photos of the completed work.

Build the draw package

Scope of work line items, GC invoices, receipts (pull the Ramp spend report, already sorted by category), and site photos.

Matt submits

Through the Genesis portal or the Kiavi process. Log it in Section C: draw number, date submitted, amount requested.

Lender inspection

The lender’s inspector visits and confirms the work is complete. Make sure the site is accessible and the work is visibly done.

Funded

The lender wires to the LLC bank account. Log the date funded and amount funded. Variance = requested minus funded.

Pay the GC

Pay the approved invoice from the LLC account and log it in Section A.

Reading the variance

Kiavi
Funded draws typically come in about $179 less than requested. That’s a draw fee. It’s expected. Log it anyway.
Genesis
If the inspector doesn’t see the work done, they fund less. Example: you request $30,000 and they fund $20,000. The $10,000 gap has to be finished, documented, and re-requested, or covered out of pocket.

Draw rules

  • Request only for work that is actually complete and photographed.
  • Log every draw the day it’s submitted and the day it’s funded.
  • Big variance? Tell Matt the same day. It means cash we planned on isn’t coming.
  • On the final draw, include out-of-pocket costs that went over earlier draws.
  • While the loan has an interest reserve, it covers the mortgage payment. After it runs out, payments come out of pocket and get tracked in Ramp.

Logging rules

  • Always record the invoice number. Catalina uses CAT-date-property (like CAT-101226-PROPERTY4). Outside subs use their own numbers. Record them exactly.
  • Never log the same invoice twice. Before adding, search the tab for the invoice number.

  • Check the ✓ column only after payment is confirmed.
Module 9

Ramp and expenses

This is the most important money skill on the team. Ramp is our company card. Salesforce is where every expense lives. The tracker’s Section D mirrors Salesforce for each property. They must all agree.

Where every dollar goes

1. Ramp card swipeReceipt captured at purchase. Auto-syncs to the LLC’s QuickBooks.
2. Classify in RampProperty, category, and draw line item.
3. Salesforce expenseEntered on the property’s deal with an expense ID like 202609-3478.
4. Tracker Section DDate, ID, description, card last 4, amount, receipt, reimbursable.

Ramp categories

CategoryExamples
MaterialsHome Depot, Lowe’s, appliances
LaborDay labor and small repairs
Permits and feesCO applications, permit fees, municipal surcharges
EquipmentTools, lockboxes, landscaping gear
OverheadUtilities, HOA, insurance, property tax
OtherPhotos, staging, cleaning, shipping

The Ramp rules

Approval first

Matt approves before you spend.

Receipt at purchase

Snap it in Ramp before you leave the store.

Right property

Every charge belongs to one deal. Never leave it unassigned.

Same day

Into Salesforce today, not end of week.

Card last 4

Record the Ramp card’s last four digits on the expense.

One place only

An expense goes in Section A or Section D, never both.

Split purchases

One receipt for several properties gets split evenly (or by what was used). Example: a $34.91 Home Depot run for three jobs becomes $11.64, $11.64, and $11.63. Write the split in the description: “Home Depot ($34.91 total for 3 properties).”

Reimbursable expenses

Some costs get paid back at closing or by someone else: prorated property tax, sewer, insurance. Mark them Reimbursable: Yes so they don’t count against profit twice.

The match test

Total spent must equal total paid On every property tab, the renovation “total spent” should match what was actually paid out. If they don’t match, something was missed or doubled. Find it before the week ends.

Common duplicates to watch for

  • A Catalina invoice logged in Section A, then the same charge entered again in Salesforce and Section D (tree removal, staging, landscaping).
  • The same invoice number entered twice in Section A.
  • A materials purchase copied onto two different properties.

Module 12

Punch list and final walk

The punch list is the last chance to catch the small things buyers notice first. Pre-plan it. Don’t wait for listing week.

  1. Touch-up paint. Scuffs, missed spots, uneven areas on walls, trim, and ceilings.
  2. Cabinets and hardware. Doors and drawers close smoothly, hardware secure and level.
  3. Caulk and grout. Tubs, backsplashes, sinks, and tile.
  4. Flooring. Scratches, gaps, uneven boards, loose tiles.
  5. Doors and closets. Close properly, don’t stick, latches work.
  1. Lights and bulbs. All fixtures working, bulbs match.
  2. Plumbing fixtures. No leaks, good pressure, drains clear.
  3. Appliances. Installed and working.
  4. Windows and screens. Open, close, lock, screens intact.
  5. Exterior. Debris gone, grading and drainage right, curb appeal.

Module 13

Make-ready, staging, and listing

The house is built. Now it has to sell fast and for top dollar. Every make-ready cost still gets logged to the property.

Post-construction cleaning

Deep clean after the trades leave. Usually a few hundred dollars per property.

Exterior and landscaping

Trash-out, grass cut, seed, cleanup. Final grass cut before winter.

Staging

Typically about $2,500 per property. Anything over budget gets flagged in the description.

Real estate photos

After staging, never before. Usually $200 to $730 depending on the shoot.

Listing details

Confirm beds, baths, and the room layout by floor. Leave building square footage off when it conflicts with the Zillow integration.

Show-ready

Lockbox on, lights working, candles and water for showings, open house prep.

Hand off to Field Ops

When it goes under contract, Field Ops starts CO and fire. Appraisal repairs come back to you.

Holding costs keep running While it’s listed, utilities, HOA, insurance, and taxes are still being paid. Every week on the market costs money, so make-ready should be planned during the punch list, not after.

What a beast does vs. what a passenger does

A beast

  • Shows up to sync knowing the status of every property they own
  • Plans the day’s route so city halls and bank runs line up
  • Fixes the problem, then reports what they did
  • Asks when unsure instead of guessing

A passenger

  • “I’m waiting to hear back” for three days straight
  • Marks a task done before it’s reviewed
  • Spends first, asks later
  • Keeps receipts in a pocket instead of the system
Non-negotiables Never spend company money without approval. Never mark a deal complete before it’s reviewed in sync. Never promise a tenant money before the budget is approved. Never enter a property that looks unsafe. Never leave a property unlocked.

Module 3

The route: contract to closing

Every deal travels the same road. Your job is to keep it moving and never let it stall at a stop. Red stops are gates: the deal can’t close until they’re cleared.

Day 0

Under contract

Deal lands in Salesforce and moves to the “Under Contract” section on Asana. The clock starts now.

Day 0 to 1

Due diligence sweep

Pull the OPRA report. Check for open permits and violations. Find out if there are tenants, an oil tank, or water meter issues. Open the town playbook.

Within 24 hours

Apply

Submit CO and fire applications. Get the right payment (check, money order, card, or cash, depending on the town). Notarize if needed. Log the expense.

Same day you get it

Get the date and schedule

Lock in inspection dates. Create the calendar invite in our format and tag the Transactions Team. Set up access with a lockbox.

Before inspection

Clear the blockers

Close open permits. Schedule tank removals. Get termite certs. Relocate tenants. Fix known violations. Check smoke and CO detectors and the extinguisher.

Inspection day

Inspect and pass

Be there. Take photos. Pass and get the certificate, or fail, get the violation list, fix it fast, and book the reinspection.

Same day

Certificates in hand

CO or TCO plus fire certificate. Upload to the deal and send to the Transactions Team.

Closing week

Closing

Every permit closed, every certificate in, every party ready. Notarized docs signed.

After closing

Close-out

Reviewed in sync, then marked complete. Lockbox pulled, utilities cancelled, signs down, property off active trackers.

The gate rule Permits must be 100% closed before closing. No exceptions without Matt’s sign-off.

Module 5

CO and fire inspections

This is the heart of the job. Most New Jersey towns won’t let a house change hands without them, so if inspections stall, the closing stalls.

The two certificates

CO (Certificate of Occupancy)
Proves the property is safe and legal to live in. Towns call it different names: CO, Continued CO, Resale Certificate, Code Compliance Certificate, or Zoning Certificate. Same idea.
Fire certificate
Proves the smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, and fire extinguisher meet state rules for a sale. Often a separate application to the fire department.
What’s a TCO? A Temporary Certificate of Occupancy. When a buyer agrees to fix items after closing, some towns allow a TCO, usually with a signed and notarized buyer affidavit (sometimes plus a termite report). Matt decides when we go the TCO route.

The standard process

1. Open the playbookEvery town is different. Check the Town Playbooks module.
›
2. Pull infoBuyer and seller details from Salesforce. IDs or LLC formation docs if required.
›
3. Pay rightCheck, money order, cashier’s check, card, or cash. Wrong payment equals a wasted trip.
›
4. SubmitOnline portal, in person, or drop box.
›
5. Get the dateThen calendar invite plus Transactions Team tagged plus expense logged.

Pre-inspection walk

Walk every property before the inspector does. Inspectors usually look for:

  • Working smoke alarms on every level and near bedrooms
  • Carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas
  • Fire extinguisher mounted near the kitchen, tagged and visible
  • House numbers visible from the street
  • Handrails on stairs
  • No exposed wiring, covers on outlets and switches
  • Utilities on, water meter accessible (some towns want a photo)
  • Water heater, furnace, and AC in working order
  • No open permits in the town’s system
  • Access ready: lockbox code works, tenants notified

Inspection day: pass or fail

Pass

  • Get the certificate (or confirm when it will be emailed)
  • Photo it and upload to the deal
  • Send to the Transactions Team
  • Update Asana and tell the sync

Fail

  • Get the violation list, in writing if you can
  • Photo every item
  • Send to Matt the same day
  • Fix fast, then book the reinspection right away
  • Log the reinspection fee
Strict inspector? Plan for it. Some inspectors are known to be tough. Walk the property twice, bring photos of fixes, and be ready to request an immediate reinspection.
Module 6

Permits, OPRA, and the blockers

The things that quietly kill closings: an old permit nobody closed, a violation on record, a buried oil tank. You find them early and clear them.

OPRA reports

OPRA (Open Public Records Act) requests let us pull a property’s records from the town: permits, violations, and history. We keep a template for towns like Paterson.

RequestSend the OPRA request as soon as the deal is under contract.
›
ReviewLook for open permits, violations, and anything odd.
›
LogNote findings on the deal with the date.
›
FlagAnything serious goes to Matt and the sync.

Open permits

An open permit is work someone pulled a permit for and never got final sign-off on. Many towns won’t issue a CO until it’s closed.

  • Find all open permits through the town or the OPRA report. John can help research.
  • If the seller pulled it, the seller (or their contractor) usually has to close it. Push them.
  • After it’s closed, the town’s system can take days to update. Call daily until it clears, then schedule the CO immediately.
  • Get closure confirmation in writing and file it on the deal.

Other common blockers

BlockerWhat you do
Oil tankConfirm if one exists. Schedule removal, get paperwork that it’s gone, and log the cost.
Termite certificateOrder or draft it (Jerson handles these). Often needed for a TCO.
Violations listGet it in writing from the town, send it to Matt, and work it down item by item.
Water meterCall the water department for service or access. Some towns want a meter photo with the application.
AffidavitsSome towns need a notarized affidavit signed by buyer and seller. Coordinate with Renee and the notary.
Illegal unit (like a basement apartment)Don’t decide alone. Bring options to Matt.
Appraisal repairsLender-required fixes before closing. Get them done, photographed, and logged.

Money: checks and expenses

We move a lot of small payments: $75 here, $150 there. Small payments are easy to lose. Losing them makes the books wrong and deals look more profitable than they are. We never rush money.

Salesforce expense rules

  • Purpose only in the label, short and specific. The property links through the deal record. Examples: CO Application Fire Inspection Fee Locksmith Service Tank Removal
  • Same day. Not end of week.
  • Check number for every check payment.
  • No duplicates. If it’s already on the renovation sheet, don’t enter it twice. When in doubt, ask Lusy.
  • Total spent must match total paid. If it doesn’t, something is missing or doubled.

Ramp card

Ramp is our company card system. Receipts attach at the moment of purchase. Your job: classify every charge so it lands on the right deal and category. If an invoice shows an account number we don’t recognize, don’t pay it. Flag it to Matt.

Know before you go Payment rules change by town. Some take cards online, some want money orders only, some take no cash, and one wants cash only for expedited inspections. The wrong payment is a wasted trip. Check the playbook and grab checks from the bank before you leave.

Module 9

Tenant relocation

Some properties come with tenants who need to move before closing. These are real people going through a stressful change. We handle it with patience and we stay within the budget.

Gather info

Check the TC notes first. If info is missing, call the tenant and get: preferred locations, monthly income, rent budget, household size, move-in timeline, moving help needed, and credit score.

Check the budget before any offer

Every relocation is different. Get approval on what we can offer from the TC or Matt before you say a number.

Set clear expectations

Tell the tenant what help we can give, the timeline, and the next steps.

Bridge options if needed

If a new place isn’t ready, offer an extended-stay hotel or a property from our Airbnb list as a temporary bridge.

Support and follow up

Check in regularly. Help with applications and paperwork. Log every conversation.

Close it out

Confirm they’re fully out before closing. Thank them. Ask for a Google review.

Calendar format
Tenant Relocation Call // 78 Milford St, City NJ ZIP // Tenant Name
Follow the law Tenants have rights. Give required notices, document everything, and never pressure or threaten. If a tenant situation gets tense or legal, stop and bring it to Matt.
Module 10

Site safety

Being a beast doesn’t mean being reckless. No deal is worth getting hurt. You’ll walk vacant houses, basements, and active job sites. These rules keep you safe.

Keep this kit in your car

  • Closed-toe work boots
  • Work gloves
  • N95 masks
  • Safety glasses
  • Flashlight (charged)
  • First aid kit
  • Phone charger or battery pack
  • Hand sanitizer and wipes
  • Photo ID and business cards
  • Folder for checks and forms

Before you go in

Tell the teamYour calendar shows where you are.
›
Walk the outsideLook for broken windows, forced doors, or signs someone is living there.
›
Knock and announceEven if it’s supposed to be vacant.
›
Watch your stepFloors, stairs, and basement steps can be rotten.

Stop and leave if…

Leave
You smell gas. Don’t touch switches. Get out, then call 911 and the gas company from outside.
Leave
Signs of a break-in or someone inside. Don’t enter. Leave and call the police, then Matt.
Leave
Water near electrical, sparking, or burning smell. Don’t touch the panel.
Back off
Suspected mold or asbestos (crumbling pipe wrap, old tile). Don’t disturb it. Mask up or step out and report it.
Back off
Aggressive dog or upset occupant. Leave calmly and reschedule.

House rules

  • No vacant property walks alone after dark.
  • Never go into tank excavations, crawl spaces, or attics without a reason and someone knowing you’re there.
  • Never enter an occupied unit without notice and permission.
  • Don’t operate electrical panels, gas valves, or boilers. That’s for licensed pros.
  • Always re-lock the property and lockbox. Don’t post lockbox codes in public places.
  • Keep checks and money orders secured, never left visible in the car.
  • Drive safe: no texting while driving. Pull over to update the system.
  • Extreme heat or cold: take breaks, keep water in the car.
If something happens Get safe first. Then tell Matt the same day with photos and what happened, even if it seems minor.
Module 11

Town playbooks

Every town runs CO and fire differently. These are pulled from our playbooks in Drive. Fees and phone numbers change, so call ahead to confirm before you go.

No town matches that search. Check the CO/FIRE folder in Drive, or ask Pat.

Patterns to notice

  • Some towns combine CO and fire into one inspection (Nutley, Piscataway).
  • Fees often rise the faster you want the inspection. Apply early, pay less.
  • Many want a notarized form. Get it notarized before the trip.
  • Almost every playbook ends the same way: get the date, calendar it, tag the Transactions Team, log the expense.
New town? Write the playbook after your first run, in the same format, and save it to the CO/FIRE folder. That’s how this list grows.
Module 12

Closing and close-out

A closed deal isn’t finished until the property is wound down. Loose ends cost money: utility bills that keep running, insurance still active, a lockbox left behind.

Before closing

  • All permits closed and finaled
  • CO or TCO and fire certificate uploaded and sent to the Transactions Team
  • Tenants fully relocated
  • Notarized documents signed
  • Every expense on the deal entered and labeled

After closing: wind-down

AreaDo this
AccessPull keys, fobs, and the lockbox. Make sure codes are changed or deactivated.
UtilitiesCancel electric, gas, water and sewer, trash, and alarm service.
VendorsTell the GC and subs the project is closed. Cancel recurring services.
InsuranceFlag to Matt so vacant or builder’s risk policies get cancelled.
MunicipalConfirm no open permits or violations left on record.
SignageRemove yard signs.
RecordsArchive photos, file certificates, take it off active trackers.
AsanaMark complete only after it’s reviewed in sync.
